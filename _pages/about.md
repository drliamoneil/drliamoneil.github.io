Welcome! My name is Liam O'Neil. I'm a Postdoctoral Research Associate with the Exercise Psychology Lab in the Department of Physical Therapy, Movement, and Rehabilitation Sciences and the Institute for Cognitive and Brain Health at Northeastern University. 

I conduct developmentally informed exercise and sport psychology research on the motivational bases of lifespan physical activity. I study the mental features guiding physical activity involvement across contexts and time, with the goal of helping people lead better and healthier lives through lifelong physical activity.

I focus much of my work on youth sport as a formative physical activity early in life. I examine what young people come to think and feel through youth sport and its motivational significance for youth sport participation. This work is aimed at better understanding youth sport experiences which are foundational to active and healthy lifestyles.

To better understand the role of youth sport in lifespan physical activity, I have a growing interest in the enduring impact of youth sport involvement on adult physical activity. This secondary focus of my research will consider the extent to which mental features emerging from and shaping youth sport participation extend to physical activity in adulthood.
