Welcome! My name is Liam O'Neil. I'm a Postdoctoral Research Associate with the Exercise Psychology Lab in the Department of Physical Therapy, Movement, and Rehabilitation Sciences and the Institute for Cognitive and Brain Health at Northeastern University. 

I conduct developmentally informed exercise and sport psychology research on the motivational bases of lifespan physical activity. Broadly, I investigate the mental features guiding physical activity involvement across contexts and time, with the goal of my research is to help people lead better and healthier lives through lifelong physical activity.

The primary arm of my research program focuses on youth sport as a popular form of physical activity among young people. As a formative experience of physical activity early in life, I examine what young people come to think and feel about themselves through youth sport and their motivational significance for youth sport participation. This work is aimed at better understanding youth sport experiences which are the foundation of an active and healthy lifestyle.

To that end, I have a growing interest in the enduring impact of youth sport on adult physical activity. This secondary arm of my research program considers the extent to which mental features emerging from and shaping youth sport participation extend to physical activity in adulthood.
