Welcome! My name is Liam O'Neil. I'm a Postdoctoral Research Associate with the Exercise Psychology Lab in the Department of Physical Therapy, Movement, and Rehabilitation Sciences and the Institute for Cognitive and Brain Health at Northeastern University. 

I'm an interdisciplinary scientist conducting developmentally informed exercise and sport psychology research on the motivational bases of lifespan physical activity. Broadly, I'm interested in what mental features guide physical activity involvement across contexts and time, and how different mental resources can boost physical activity engagement. The ultimate goal of my research is to help people lead better and healthier lives through lifelong physical activity.

I'm particularly passionate about youth sport as a formative physical activity early in life. To that end, much of my work has focused on what young people come to think and feel about themselves and physical activity through youth sport, and how those mental features impact the youth sport experience. I'm committed to promoting quality youth sport experiences that set the stage for an active and healthy lifestyle.

As an extension to this work, I have a growing interest in the extent to which mental features shaping the youth sport experience translate to youth and adult exercise. This includes questions related to the continuity or change of motivationally salient mental features from youth sport to adult exercise and the extent to which memories of youth sport are.
