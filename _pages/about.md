Welcome! My name is Liam O'Neil. I'm a Postdoctoral Research Associate with the Exercise Psychology Lab in the Department of Physical Therapy, Movement, and Rehabilitation Sciences and the Institute for Cognitive and Brain Health at Northeastern University. 

I conduct developmentally informed exercise and sport psychology research on the motivational bases of lifespan physical activity. The broad focus of my work is on understanding the mental features guiding physical activity involvement across contexts and time, and the mental resources and strategies that can boost physical activity engagement. The ultimate goal of my research is to help people lead better and healthier lives through lifelong physical activity.

There are two arms of my research program that focus on critical junctures for physical activity involvement. The first and established arm of my research focuses on early life and the role of youth sport as a formative physical activity. Although youth sport is a popular form of physical activity among young people, youth sport dropout is a common occurrence and contributes to declining levels of physical activity from childhood and adolescence to adulthood. To address this problem, I investigate what mental features have motivational significance for youth sport participation, how motivationally significant mental features change from childhood to adolscence and shape youth sport participation, how mental resources that can be developed through youth sport to promote engagement with this and other physical activities. In doing so, my work can help curb the youth sport dropout crisis and young people sustain their physical activity involvement into adulthood.

A second arm of my research and growing interest of mine is on late life as another period of declining physical activity.
