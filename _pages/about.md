Welcome! My name is Liam O'Neil. I'm a Postdoctoral Research Associate with the Exercise Psychology Lab in the Department of Physical Therapy, Movement, and Rehabilitation Sciences and the Institute for Cognitive and Brain Health at Northeastern University. 

I conduct developmentally informed exercise and sport psychology research on the motivational bases of lifespan physical activity. The broad focus of my work is on understanding what mental features guide physical activity involvement across contexts and time, and how mental resources and strategies can boost physical activity engagement. The ultimate goal of my research is to help people lead better and healthier lives through lifelong physical activity.

I'm most passionate about the role of youth sport as a formative physical activity early in life. Although youth sport is a popular form of physical activity among young people, youth sport dropout is common and contributes to declining levels of physical activity into adulthood. To address this problem, I investigate what mental features have motivational significance for youth sport participation, how motivationally salient mental features shape youth sport participation, and how mental resources can be developed through youth sport to promote engagement with this and other physical activities. This research is aimed at promoting quality youth sport experiences that translate into active and healthy lifestyles in adulthood.
