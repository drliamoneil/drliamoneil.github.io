Welcome! My name is Liam O'Neil. I'm a Postdoctoral Research Associate with the Exercise Psychology Lab in the Department of Physical Therapy, Movement, and Rehabilitation Sciences and the Institute for Cognitive and Brain Health at Northeastern University. 

I'm an interdisciplinary scientist conducting developmentally informed exercise and sport psychology research on the motivational bases of lifespan physical activity. Broadly, I study the mental features guiding physical activity involvement across contexts and time, and mental resources can boost physical activity engagement. The ultimate goal of my research is to help people lead better and healthier lives through lifelong physical activity.

I'm passionate about unpacking the role of youth sport as a formative physical activity early in life. To that end, much of my work has focused on what young people come to understand about themselves and their relationship with physical activity through youth sport. I'm committed to promoting quality youth sport experiences that set the stage for an active and healthy lifestyle.

To better understand the impact of youth sport on lifelong physical activity, I have a growing interest in whether mental features shaping the youth sport experience extend to adult physical activity. This includes questions related to the continuity or change of motivationally salient mental features from youth sport to adult physical activity.
