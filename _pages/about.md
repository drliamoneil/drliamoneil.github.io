Welcome! My name is Liam O'Neil. I'm a Postdoctoral Research Associate with the Exercise Psychology Lab in the Department of Physical Therapy, Movement, and Rehabilitation Sciences and the Institute for Cognitive and Brain Health at Northeastern University. 

I conduct developmentally informed exercise and sport psychology research on the motivational bases of lifespan physical activity. The broad focus of my work is on understanding the mental features guiding physical activity involvement across contexts and time, and the mental resources and strategies that can boost physical activity engagement. The ultimate goal of my research is to help people lead better and healthier lives through lifelong physical activity.

I'm especially passionate about youth sport as a formative physical activity early in life. Although youth sport is a popular form of physical activity among young people, youth sport dropout is a common occurrence and contributing factor to declining levels of physical activity from early to midlife. To address this problem, the primary arm of my research program investigates what mental features with motivational significance for youth sport participation and how mental resources that can be developed through youth sport to promote engagement with this and other physical activities. In doing so, I hope to curb the youth sport dropout crisis and help young people sustain their physical activity involvement into adulthood.
